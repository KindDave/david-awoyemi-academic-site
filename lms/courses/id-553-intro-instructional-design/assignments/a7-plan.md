Produce the instructional plan for your course design project. This is the major deliverable of the course; Parts 2 and 3 build on it. It integrates everything from Assignments 2, 5, and 6 and the Objective Clinic.

## Components

### 1. Front matter (from earlier work, revised)

- Project title, intended learners, and setting.
- The instructional problem and needs assessment summary (one page).
- The ID model or hybrid you are following and why (one paragraph, revised from Assignment 2).
- Summary of learner and context analysis with the implications table (revised from Assignment 5).
- Task analysis diagram with entry-skill line (revised from Assignment 6).

### 2. Objectives and assessment plan

- Terminal objective and all enabling objectives, numbered to the task analysis, in Mager or ABCD format (revised from the Objective Clinic).
- For each objective: assessment type, a sample item or task description, and the scoring approach. Include at least one performance assessment with an analytic rubric.
- An alignment table: objective, outcome type, assessment, and the lesson(s) that teach it.

### 3. Instructional strategy

- Overall sequence and structure: lessons or units, duration, delivery mode, grouping.
- For each lesson: objectives covered; the nine events with a sentence on how each is handled; practice and feedback design; media with a one-sentence justification tied to the event it serves.
- A Merrill's First Principles check for the whole plan (one paragraph per principle).
- A cognitive load note: what extraneous load you removed and how you managed intrinsic load.

### 4. Multimedia and accessibility

- Media asset list for the storyboarded lesson, with the Mayer principles governing each asset.
- Two or three UDL checkpoints applied, with the specific design feature for each.
- Accessibility checklist completed for the storyboarded lesson.
- Tools you would use to develop the assets, and why.

### 5. Storyboard and prototype

- A complete storyboard for one lesson (15 to 40 screens or segments) using the fields on the prototyping page.
- A low-fidelity prototype of at least one interaction (a branching scenario, a practice activity, or a simulation), delivered as clickable slides, a Twine or H5P link, a paper prototype photographed, or an authoring-tool draft.
- A short note on what you learned from building the prototype.

### 6. Implementation notes

- What facilitators, learners, and administrators need in order to run the instruction (a facilitator guide outline, learner instructions, technical requirements).
- Project timeline and resources for full development, in a simple table.

## Format

A single PDF or Google Doc with a table of contents, plus a link or attachment for the prototype. Expect 15 to 30 pages including tables and the storyboard. APA 7. AI-use disclosure if applicable.
