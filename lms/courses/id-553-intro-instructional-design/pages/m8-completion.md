## Build your portfolio

Your instructional plan, storyboard, prototype, and evaluation plan are portfolio pieces. Before you file them away:

- Remove any identifying information about the learners you studied.
- Write a one-page project summary: the problem, your process, the design, and what the evaluation would show. Hiring managers read the summary, not the 30 pages.
- Export the prototype in a form that will still open in two years (PDF of the storyboard, a video walkthrough of the interaction).

## Where to go next

- **Advanced coursework.** Courses in e-learning development (authoring tools, SCORM and xAPI), evaluation and assessment, learning analytics, and design-based research build directly on this one.
- **Professional community.** The Association for Educational Communications and Technology (AECT) and the Association for Talent Development (ATD) publish standards, journals, and conference programs. AECT's *Educational Technology Research and Development* is the field's primary journal.
- **Standards.** Review the IBSTPI instructional designer competencies and rate yourself; the gaps are your next learning objectives.
- **Practice.** The fastest way to get better at instructional design is to design instruction, evaluate it, and revise it. Volunteer for a project in your organization within the next three months.
