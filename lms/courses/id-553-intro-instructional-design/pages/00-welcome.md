Welcome to ID 553. This course introduces the principles and practice of instructional design (ID): the systematic process of analyzing learning needs, designing and developing instruction, and evaluating whether it worked. You will leave with a working knowledge of the major ID models, experience analyzing realistic design cases, and a complete instructional plan of your own.

## Course description

An intensive introduction to the principles associated with designing instruction for classroom, workplace, and online settings. Topics include the history and models of instructional design, front-end analysis (learner, context, and task), learning objectives and aligned assessment, instructional strategies, multimedia and accessibility principles, rapid prototyping, and formative and summative evaluation. Two case analyses develop professional judgment; a staged instructional plan develops practical skill. Three credit hours.

## Who this course is for

- Teachers who want to design instruction more deliberately.
- Trainers and faculty developers who build courses and workshops.
- Technologists and media specialists who work with subject-matter experts.
- Graduate students preparing for advanced ID coursework or research.

No prior ID coursework is assumed.

## Course objectives

By the end of this course you will be able to:

1. Describe the origins, purposes, and major models of instructional design and select a model appropriate to a given context.
2. Analyze an instructional design case, identify the underlying design problem, and recommend a defensible course of action.
3. Conduct learner, context, and task analyses for a real instructional need.
4. Write measurable learning objectives and design assessments aligned with them.
5. Plan an instructional intervention that applies evidence-based strategies and multimedia and accessibility principles.
6. Design a formative and summative evaluation for the intervention.
7. Reflect critically on your own design decisions and on the limits of the models you use.
