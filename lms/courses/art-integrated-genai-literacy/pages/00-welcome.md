Dear participant,

Welcome to a learning experience where creativity meets technology. You are about to build generative AI (GenAI) literacy the way artists learn best: by making things. Over two modules and eight lessons you will compose songs, produce beats, score scenes, write and stage scripts, direct avatars, and choreograph movement, all with GenAI tools as your collaborators. Along the way you will learn to understand, evaluate, and ethically guide those tools rather than simply use them.

## Why this course exists

The next generation of learners will grow up in an AI-driven world. As a future music or theater teacher, your ability to navigate, harness, and ethically guide the use of AI will shape how your students create. By building GenAI literacy now, you will:

- Inspire your future students to become creative digital citizens.
- Enhance your own teaching practice with current tools.
- Lead classroom innovation through technology-enhanced arts experiences.
- Understand the ethics, limits, and possibilities of AI in education.

## How the course is organized

| Part | What you do | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Module 0: Orientation | Set up accounts, meet your peers, take the self-assessment | 45 minutes |
| Module 1: Music | Four lessons, each ending in a shareable audio artifact | 4 to 6 hours |
| Module 2: Theater | Four lessons, each ending in a shareable video or script artifact | 4 to 6 hours |
| Module 3: Your Classroom | Design a lesson plan that brings one project to your students | 2 to 3 hours |

You may complete Module 1 and Module 2 in either order. If your time is limited, complete the module closest to your teaching area first, then return for the other.

## What every lesson looks like

Each lesson follows the same rhythm so you always know what comes next:

1. **Watch** a short introductory video and read the lesson objectives.
2. **Learn** the key ideas behind the tool and the competency the lesson targets.
3. **Do** a step-by-step activity with the GenAI tool.
4. **Create** a shareable digital artifact (a file or a link).
5. **Share** the artifact in the lesson gallery with a rationale of no more than 75 words.
6. **Check** your understanding with a short knowledge check.
7. **Reflect** with two or three prompts at the end of the lesson.

## What you need

- A laptop or desktop computer with a reliable internet connection and headphones.
- Free accounts for the tools listed on the "Tools, Accounts, and Setup" page.
- A place to store your artifacts (Google Drive, OneDrive, or a personal folder) so you can link to them.
- Curiosity and a willingness to make imperfect first drafts.

## How you are assessed

This course is competency-based. There are no timed exams.

| Component | Weight | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lesson galleries (8 artifacts with rationales) | 40% | Complete/incomplete, with instructor feedback |
| Knowledge checks (8 short quizzes) | 10% | Unlimited attempts, best score kept |
| Module reflections (2) | 20% | Rubric-graded |
| Final project: lesson plan | 30% | Rubric-graded, with peer feedback |

A score of 80% or higher earns a certificate of completion.
