## Design principles for arts-integrated GenAI lessons

1. **Art first, tool second.** The learning objective is a music or theater outcome (compose a 16-bar phrase, score a monologue into beats). The GenAI tool is the medium, and the GenAI competency is the secondary objective.
2. **Make the human contribution visible.** Every lesson has a step the tool cannot do: the lyric, the beat sheet, the rewrite, the failure diagnosis. Assess that step.
3. **Iterate on purpose.** Build in at least one "change one variable" cycle so students learn what the prompt controls.
4. **Evaluate with criteria.** Give students a small grid (three to six criteria) to judge outputs. Never accept "I like it."
5. **Disclose and credit.** Require the one-line AI credit on every artifact. Model it yourself.
6. **Plan for no tool.** Have a low-tech path for students without access, a blocked network, or a tool that fails on the day.
7. **Ask the ethics question.** Every lesson includes one moment where students consider consent, ownership, representation, or disclosure.

## Lesson plan template

Copy this into a document. Fill every section.

| Section | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| Title and level | Lesson title; grade band; class type (general music, choir, band, drama, technical theater) |
| Duration | Total minutes and number of sessions |
| Standards | One arts standard (National Core Arts Standards) and one technology or AI literacy standard (ISTE Standards for Students, or the competency framework from this course) |
| Objectives | Two or three, written as "Students will be able to..." with an observable verb; label each as art outcome or GenAI competency |
| Materials and tools | Tools with free-tier confirmation; accounts needed; low-tech alternative |
| Hook (5 minutes) | A warm-up that surfaces the art concept before the tool appears |
| Direct instruction (10 minutes) | The key idea, in student language, with one worked example |
| Guided practice (15 to 20 minutes) | Step-by-step activity with the tool; the "one variable" cycle; the evaluation grid |
| Independent creation (15 to 20 minutes) | The artifact students make; the human-only step |
| Share and reflect (10 minutes) | How students share (gallery, pair swap, class listen); two reflection prompts |
| Assessment | A rubric with three or four criteria; at least one criterion assesses the human-only step |
| Ethics moment | The question students consider, and when |
| Differentiation | Supports for emerging learners; extensions for advanced; accommodation for students without access |
| AI credit | The credit line students will attach |

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Objectives that describe the tool ("Students will use Suno") rather than the learning.
- Rubrics that grade the AI's output quality instead of the student's decisions.
- No plan for the day the tool is down.
- Skipping the ethics moment because "there isn't time."
