# Assessment Alignment and Rubrics

*ID 553 - Module 4: Objectives and Aligned Assessment*

Draft narration script. Total running time about 5 min 55 s.

## Title slide

Assessment Alignment and Rubrics. ID 553 - Module 4: Objectives and Aligned Assessment. Presented by Idowu David Awoyemi, The University of Alabama.

## Slide 2: Assessment comes before strategy

- Dick and Carey: assessments right after objectives, before strategies
- Backward Design: decide acceptable evidence before planning activities
- Aligned means the performance, the conditions, and the standard all match the objective
- Check all three, every time

In Dick and Carey, assessment instruments are developed immediately after objectives and before instructional strategies. Backward Design makes the same move: decide what evidence you'll accept before you plan activities. The reason is alignment. If the assessment is designed last, it measures what was convenient to teach rather than what the objective required, and learners learn to the test rather than to the performance. An assessment is aligned when the performance it elicits, the conditions it imposes, and the standard it applies all match the objective. Check all three, every time.

## Slide 3: Types by purpose; criterion-referenced

- Entry-skill test, pretest, embedded practice checks, posttest, performance assessment
- Every project needs the last two; most benefit from embedded checks
- Criterion-referenced: each learner against the standard in the objective, not against other learners
- If learners fail in large numbers, the instruction is the problem

Assessments serve different purposes at different times: entry-skill tests, pretests, embedded practice checks, posttests, and performance assessments. Every project needs the last two, and most benefit from embedded checks, because retrieval practice improves retention. All of them are criterion-referenced. Each learner is measured against the standard in the objective, not against other learners. Grading on a curve makes no sense when the question is whether a nurse can administer medication safely. If learners fail in large numbers, the instruction is the problem, not the learners.

## Slide 4: One objective, three aligned items

- Objective: Given a mismatch alert, the nurse will decide whether to stop and state the reason
- Aligned 1: six new alert scenarios; decide stop or proceed, with a rationale
- Aligned 2: a branching simulation in the practice environment with alerts at two points
- Aligned 3: observed performance on a checklist during a simulated administration

Take one objective: given a mismatch alert, the nurse will decide whether to stop and state the reason. Three aligned assessments. First, six new alert scenarios the learner hasn't seen, deciding in each whether to stop, with a rationale. Second, a branching simulation in the practice environment that throws alerts at two points. Third, observed performance on a checklist during a simulated administration. All three present a decision, because the objective is a decision, and all three use new situations rather than the examples used in instruction.

## Slide 5: The same objective, three misaligned items

- Misaligned 1: 'Define mismatch alert.' Tests recall, not decision
- Misaligned 2: 'List the three alert types.' Verbal information for a rule-use objective
- Misaligned 3: 'Describe the alert workflow in your own words.' Description is not performance
- The most common error: testing a rule-use objective with a recall item

Now three misaligned items for the same objective. 'Define mismatch alert' tests recall, not decision. 'List the three alert types' tests verbal information when the objective is rule use. 'Describe the alert workflow in your own words' asks for description, and describing a workflow is not the same as performing it under pressure. The most common alignment error in the field is exactly this: testing a rule-use objective with a recall item. If the objective says decide, the assessment must present a decision.

## Slide 6: Matching item types to outcomes

- Verbal information: short answer, matching, multiple choice
- Concepts: classify new examples and non-examples
- Rules and problem solving: new problems, scenarios, cases with a decision
- Motor skills: observed performance with a checklist
- Attitudes: observed choice in realistic situations; self-report only as a supplement
- Create-level objectives: products judged with a rubric

Match the item type to the outcome type. Verbal information suits short answer, matching, and multiple choice. Concepts need classification of new examples and non-examples, not recall of the definition. Rules and problem solving need new problems, scenarios, and cases with a decision. Motor skills need observed performance with a checklist, not a written description of the procedure. Attitudes need observed choices in realistic situations; a quiz on what the right attitude is tells you nothing. And create-level objectives need products judged with a rubric.

## Slide 7: Writing selected-response items

- The stem states a complete problem; the learner can answer before reading options
- Options parallel in length and grammar; the right one is not the longest
- Distractors come from the common errors in the task analysis
- No 'all of the above'; no negatives in the stem
- One item, one objective; tag each item with its objective number

When you do write multiple choice, follow the rules. The stem states a complete problem, so the learner could answer before reading the options. Options are parallel in length and grammar, and the correct answer isn't the longest. Distractors are plausible and, ideally, drawn from the common errors you recorded in the task analysis; a distractor nobody chooses is wasted. Avoid 'all of the above' and negatives in the stem. And tag each item with its objective number so you can analyze results by objective later.

## Slide 8: Building an analytic rubric

- Checklists for procedures; rating scales are fast but unreliable across raters
- Analytic rubric: each criterion described at each level
- 1. List three to six criteria from the objective. 2. Describe exemplary. 3. Describe beginning. 4. Fill the middle by naming what is present and missing
- 5. Test with a second rater on real samples; revise where you disagree

For performance and product objectives, the assessment is a task plus a scoring guide. Checklists suit procedures with a correct sequence. Rating scales are fast but unreliable across raters. Analytic rubrics, which describe each criterion at each level of performance, are the most reliable and give the most useful feedback. Build one in five steps: list three to six criteria from the objective and task analysis; describe the exemplary level in observable terms; describe the beginning level; fill in the middle by naming what's present and what's missing; then test the rubric with a second rater on two or three real samples and revise wherever you disagree. Every assignment rubric in this course was built that way.

## Slide 9: Feedback, validity, reliability, fairness

- Feedback: specific to the criterion, says what to do next, arrives in time to act
- Validity: the assessment measures the objective; alignment is the main check
- Reliability: enough items per objective, clear rubrics, rater training
- Fairness: no disadvantage unrelated to the objective; multiple means of expression where the objective allows
- Draft the assessment for your terminal objective and bring it to the clinic

Three last things. Assessment during instruction is only formative if learners get feedback they can use: specific to the criterion, telling them what to do next, arriving while they can still act. Validity means the assessment measures the objective, and alignment is the main check. Reliability means consistent results across items, occasions, and raters; enough items, clear rubrics, and rater training are the levers. Fairness means the assessment doesn't disadvantage learners for reasons unrelated to the objective, so offer multiple means of expression where the objective allows. Draft the assessment for your terminal objective now and bring it to the objective clinic with your objectives.
