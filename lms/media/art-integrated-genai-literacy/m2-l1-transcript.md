# Lesson 2.1: AI-Assisted Dramatic Storytelling

*GENAI 101 - Module 2: Theater*

Draft narration script. Total running time about 2 min 36 s.

## Title slide

Lesson 2.1: AI-Assisted Dramatic Storytelling. GENAI 101 - Module 2: Theater. Presented by Idowu David Awoyemi, The University of Alabama.

## Slide 2: What you will do

- Write a scene brief: characters, objective, obstacle, setting, tone, form
- Draft a two-page scene with a text model
- Direct two rounds of revision with specific notes
- Rewrite at least a third of the lines yourself and prepare a table read

In this lesson you'll write a two-character scene with a text model as your writing partner. You'll write a brief, get a first draft, direct two rounds of revision with specific notes, and then rewrite at least a third of the dialogue in your own voice. The finished scene, marked to show your changes, goes into the gallery ready for a table read.

## Slide 3: The scene brief

- Characters: who, how old, what relationship?
- Objective: what does each want in this scene?
- Obstacle: what stands in the way?
- Setting, tone, and form: where, how it should feel, how long

A text model is a fast, tireless, slightly bland writing partner. Its first draft will be competent and forgettable. What makes it useful is a brief that carries your artistic choices. Who are the characters and what is their relationship? What does each one want in this scene? What stands in the way? Where are we, what should it feel like, and how long should it be? Objective meeting obstacle is the core of dramatic writing, and the model doesn't know what your characters want unless you tell it.

## Slide 4: Prompting is directing

- Vague: 'make it better', 'more emotional'
- Specific: 'Cut the first six lines; start with her already holding the trumpet case'
- 'Give him one line where he almost agrees, then retreats'
- 'Replace the final speech with a single gesture'

After the first draft, your prompts become director's notes. Good notes are specific and actionable. 'Cut the exposition in the first six lines and start with her already holding the trumpet case.' 'Give him one line where he almost agrees, then retreats.' 'Replace the final speech with a single gesture.' Vague notes like 'make it better' produce vague rewrites. You'll give three notes per round, two rounds. There's a note bank on the lesson page to adapt.

## Slide 5: The human rewrite

- Models write characters who say what they mean and explain their feelings
- Real people deflect, interrupt, joke at the wrong moment, leave things unsaid
- Cut explanation, add subtext, give each character one verbal habit
- Mark your changes so a reader can see them

A scene is finished when it sounds like people you know. Models default to characters who say exactly what they mean and explain their feelings. Real people deflect, interrupt, joke at the wrong moment, and leave things unsaid. Your rewrite is where the scene becomes yours: cut explanation, add subtext, and give each character one verbal habit. Track your changes so a reader can see what you did. Then add a title, a character list, and a one-line note to actors, and post it with your brief, your six notes, and your rationale. Read it aloud before you post; you'll hear things you can't see.
