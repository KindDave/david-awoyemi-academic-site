## Slide 3: Title, level, duration, standards

- Title: Patterns You Can Hear: Building a Beat
- Grade 7 general music; 50 minutes, one session
- Arts standard: National Core Arts Standards, Music, Creating (Cr1, Cr2)
- Technology standard: ISTE Students 5, Computational Thinker; course competency: computational thinking

Start with the basics. A title in student language, the grade band, the class type, and the duration. Then two standards: one arts standard, here the National Core Arts Standards for creating music, and one technology or AI literacy standard, here the ISTE computational thinker standard, which lines up with the course's computational thinking competency. The arts standard comes first. The tool is the medium, not the point.

## Slide 4: Objectives with observable verbs

- Art outcome: Students will build a four-bar drum pattern on a 16-cell grid and extend it to 16 bars with at least two variations
- GenAI competency: Students will describe what an AI loop recommendation decided and state whether they kept it and why
- Label each objective by type

Write two or three objectives with observable verbs, and label each as an art outcome or a GenAI competency. For our example: students will build a four-bar drum pattern on a sixteen-cell grid and extend it to sixteen bars with at least two variations. That's the art outcome. And: students will describe what an AI loop recommendation decided and state whether they kept it and why. That's the competency. Notice that neither objective says 'students will use Soundtrap'. Objectives describe learning, not tools.

## Slide 5: Hook, instruction, guided practice

- Hook (5 min): clap the kick-snare-hi-hat pattern with no computer
- Direct instruction (10 min): the grid on the board; one worked example
- Guided practice (15 min): build the four-bar pattern together; change one cell and listen
- Evaluation grid: three criteria students use to judge their own loop

The hook surfaces the art concept before the tool appears: the class claps the kick, snare, and hi-hat pattern by hand. Direct instruction puts the grid on the board and walks through one worked example. Guided practice builds the four-bar pattern together, then changes one cell at a time and listens to what happened. That's the one-variable cycle from the course, and it's where students learn what the grid actually controls. Give them a small evaluation grid, three criteria, to judge their own loop. Never accept 'I like it'.

## Slide 6: Independent creation and the human-only step

- Independent creation (15 min): extend to 16 bars with two variations; try loop recommendation
- Human-only step: the written list of variations and the sentence about the AI's suggestion
- Share and reflect (5 min): pair swap; two prompts
- Assessment: rubric with three criteria; one assesses the human-only step

Independent creation is where students extend to sixteen bars, add two variations, and try the loop recommendation feature. The human-only step, the part the tool cannot do, is the written list of variations and the sentence about what the AI suggested and whether they kept it. Your rubric must assess that step; otherwise you're grading the software. Share with a pair swap and two reflection prompts. Keep the rubric to three or four criteria.

## Slide 7: Ethics moment, access, credit

- Ethics moment: 'Where do the loops in the library come from? Who made them?' (2 minutes, during guided practice)
- Low-tech path: paper grid and body percussion; one shared device per group
- Differentiation: pre-filled grid for emerging learners; a second instrument layer for advanced
- AI credit: 'Loops recommended by [tool]; pattern and variations by [student]'

Three sections people skip. The ethics moment: two minutes during guided practice asking where the loops in the library come from and who made them. The low-tech path: a paper grid and body percussion, with one shared device per group, so the lesson survives a blocked network or a tool that's down. Differentiation: a pre-filled grid for emerging learners and a second instrument layer for advanced ones. And the AI credit line students attach to their work.
