# Lesson 2.3: Digital Choreography and Drama-Dance

*GENAI 101 - Module 2: Theater*

Draft narration script. Total running time about 3 min 27 s.

## Title slide

Lesson 2.3: Digital Choreography and Drama-Dance. GENAI 101 - Module 2: Theater. Presented by Idowu David Awoyemi, The University of Alabama.

## Slide 2: What you will do

- Write a movement score: four phrases using body, action, space, time, energy
- Generate each phrase with a text-to-video tool; log every failure and fix
- Assemble a 30 to 60 second piece using a choreographic device
- Write a transfer note: how you would teach it to live bodies

In this lesson you'll choreograph a short movement piece from generated video. You'll write a movement score of four phrases, generate each phrase, keep a log of what failed and how you fixed it, assemble the phrases with a choreographic device, and write a note about how the piece would be taught to live dancers or actors. Expect the tool to fail. That's why this lesson builds problem solving.

## Slide 3: The elements of dance as a prompt frame

- Body: which parts lead, what shape?
- Action: reach, collapse, spiral, freeze
- Space: level, direction, pathway, size
- Time: tempo, rhythm, duration
- Energy: heavy and bound, sudden and light

Text-to-video models respond to the same specificity that music and text models do, and dance educators already have the vocabulary. Body: which parts lead, what shape? Action: the movement verbs. Space: level, direction, pathway, size. Time: tempo, rhythm, duration. Energy: the quality of the movement. A prompt that names all five, like 'one dancer, arms leading a slow backward spiral at low level, heavy and bound, then a sudden light reach upward and freeze, five seconds', gives the model something to render. 'A sad dance' does not.

## Slide 4: Expect failure, then diagnose

- Bodies drift, hands multiply, tempo gets ignored
- Fix 1: revise the prompt. One dancer, one action, one quality, single shot, a duration
- Fix 2: change the tool or mode. Image-to-video, or character animation
- Fix 3: edit the output. Trim to the usable two seconds, slow it, mirror it

Text-to-video is the least reliable tool in this course. Bodies drift, hands multiply, and the model may ignore your tempo. For every unusable clip, name the failure and pick a fix. First, simplify the prompt: one dancer, one action, one quality, a single continuous shot, a stated duration. Second, change the tool or the mode; try image-to-video from a still you make, or switch to character animation for precise control. Third, edit the output: trim to the two usable seconds, slow it down, mirror it, cut on the beat. Your failure-and-fix log is part of the submission, because the diagnosis is the learning.

## Slide 5: Composition is yours

- The model makes phrases; you make the piece
- Repetition, canon, contrast, accumulation
- Four phrases arranged with intention is a composition; four phrases in a row is a slideshow

The model makes phrases. You make the piece. Choreographic devices like repetition, canon, contrast, and accumulation are decisions about sequence and relationship, and they belong to you. Four generated phrases arranged with intention is a composition. The same four phrases dumped in order is a slideshow. Choose your device before you generate, and let it shape the prompts.

## Slide 6: Back to the room

- Three sentences: how would you teach these four phrases to live bodies?
- What did the video show you that improvisation would not have?
- Consider the safety, consent, and body-image conversations a classroom would need

Finish with a transfer note. In three sentences, describe how you'd teach these four phrases to live dancers or actors in a rehearsal room, and what the video showed you that improvisation wouldn't have. When you reflect, think about the conversations this activity would require in a classroom: safety, consent, and body image among them. Then export with title cards naming each phrase, post it with your score and your log, and tell two classmates which device you see in their piece and how it could be performed live.
