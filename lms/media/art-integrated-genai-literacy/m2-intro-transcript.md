Draft narration script. Total running time about 2 min 31 s.

## Title slide

Theater Module Introduction. GENAI 101 - Module 2: Theater. Presented by Idowu David Awoyemi, The University of Alabama.

## Slide 2: What you will make in this module

- Lesson 2.1: a two-character scene, drafted with a text model and rewritten by you
- Lesson 2.2: a 45 to 90 second avatar performance of a monologue
- Lesson 2.3: a 30 to 60 second drama-dance piece from text-to-video phrases
- Lesson 2.4: a critique of AI feedback and a position on staging AI-assisted work

Welcome to the theater module. Four lessons, four artifacts. In lesson two point one, you'll draft a two-character scene with a text model, direct two rounds of revision, and rewrite a third of the lines yourself. In two point two, you'll score a monologue into beats and build an avatar performance of it. In two point three, you'll choreograph a short movement piece from generated video phrases. And in two point four, you'll evaluate AI feedback on your own scene and take a position on whether you would stage AI-assisted work with students.

## Slide 3: The competencies behind the projects

- 2.1 Prompt engineering, creativity and innovation
- 2.2 Computational thinking, collaboration
- 2.3 Problem solving, creativity and innovation
- 2.4 AI feedback evaluation, AI ethics literacy

The competencies map the same way they did in music. Storytelling is prompt engineering and creativity: a scene brief, director's notes, and a human rewrite. Avatar performance is computational thinking: decomposing a speech into beats and sequencing four variables per beat. Choreography is problem solving: text-to-video is the least reliable tool in the course, so you'll diagnose failures and fix them. And the final lesson is evaluation and ethics.

## Slide 4: Same method, new vocabulary

- A brief before you generate: characters, objective, obstacle, setting, tone, form
- One variable per iteration; specific director's notes, not 'make it better'
- Evaluate against criteria: useful, generic, or wrong
- Credit the tool and your contribution on every artifact

If you completed the music module, you'll recognize the moves. Write a brief before you generate. Change one thing at a time. Evaluate against criteria you named in advance. Credit the tool. Theater changes the vocabulary, not the method. Objective and obstacle replace tempo and instrumentation; director's notes replace prompt revisions; beats replace bars.

## Slide 5: A note on the live art

- Theater happens between people in a room; nothing here replaces that
- The tools help you draft faster, visualize staging, rehearse without a partner
- Every lesson asks how the artifact returns to the rehearsal room
- Keep a Theater Module Notes document: every prompt, one line on the result

One thing before you start. Theater happens between people in a room, and nothing in this module replaces that. The tools help you draft faster, visualize staging before you have a cast, rehearse without a partner, and give students a way to see their ideas move. Every lesson asks how the artifact returns to the rehearsal room. Keep a Theater Module Notes document with every prompt you use and one line about the result. Let's go to lesson two point one.
