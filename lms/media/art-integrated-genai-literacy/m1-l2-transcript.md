In this lesson you'll build a sixteen-bar beat in a browser-based workstation. You'll break the beat into layers, write each layer as a repeating pattern, extend it to sixteen bars with deliberate variations, and use one of the tool's AI features. Then you'll share the beat and give a classmate structured feedback on their arrangement.

## Slide 3: Beats are patterns, and patterns are code

- Decomposition: break the groove into instruments
- Pattern recognition: the hi-hat repeats every eighth note
- Abstraction: the groove becomes a grid
- Algorithm design: sequence bars into a form

Computational thinking has four moves, and a drum loop uses all four at once. Decomposition: you break the groove into kick, snare, hi-hat, bass, and chords. Pattern recognition: you notice the hi-hat repeats every eighth note and the snare lands on two and four. Abstraction: you reduce the groove to a grid of cells. And algorithm design: you sequence the bars into a form with an ordered list of changes.

## Slide 4: The grid

- One bar of 4/4 = 16 sixteenth-note cells
- Kick on cells 1 and 9; snare on 5 and 13; hi-hat on every odd cell
- That is the most common pattern in popular music; everything else is variation

Loop-based tools show time as a grid: bars across the top, instruments down the side. One bar of four-four has sixteen cells. Put the kick on cells one and nine, the snare on five and thirteen, and a hi-hat on every odd cell, and you have the most common pattern in popular music. Almost everything else you've ever danced to is a variation on that grid. Write your pattern on paper first; that's your decomposition.

## Slide 5: Form is repetition with variation

- A 16-bar beat is usually one 4-bar phrase, four times, with small changes
- A fill at bar 4, drop the bass at bar 8, new element at bar 9, a pause before bar 13
- Listeners hear repetition as stability and variation as interest

A sixteen-bar beat is not sixteen different bars. It's usually one four-bar phrase repeated four times with small changes: a fill at the end of bar four, the bass dropping out in bar eight, a new element arriving at bar nine, a one-beat silence before bar thirteen. Listeners hear the repetition as stability and the variation as interest. Your numbered list of those changes is your algorithm.

## Slide 6: Where the AI is

- Loop recommendation: suggests loops that fit your key and tempo
- Pattern generation: proposes a drum pattern in a style
- Automatic mastering: balances levels and loudness
- Each is a decision made for you. Notice it. Decide whether you agree.

Modern workstations hide AI in several places. Loop recommendation suggests loops that fit your key and tempo. Pattern generation proposes a drum pattern in a chosen style. Automatic mastering balances levels and loudness. Each of these is a decision made for you. Your job in this lesson is to use at least one, listen with it and without it, notice what it decided, and decide whether you agree. Write that down; it's the heart of your rationale. When you're done, export the beat, post it with your pattern grid and your list of variations, and reply to two peers using the feedback frame in the lesson page.
