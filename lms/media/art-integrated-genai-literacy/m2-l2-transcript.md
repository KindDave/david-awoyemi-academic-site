# Lesson 2.2: Avatar-Based Performance

*GENAI 101 - Module 2: Theater*

Draft narration script. Total running time about 2 min 46 s.

## Title slide

Lesson 2.2: Avatar-Based Performance. GENAI 101 - Module 2: Theater. Presented by Idowu David Awoyemi, The University of Alabama.

## Slide 2: What you will do

- Score a 45 to 90 second monologue into 5 to 8 beats
- Fill a beat sheet: gesture, expression, camera, voice for each beat
- Build one animated scene per beat in Animaker
- Record your own voice for at least one beat and compare

In this lesson you'll direct an avatar. You'll take a short monologue, score it into beats, decide what the performer does in each beat, and build it in an animation tool one scene at a time. You'll use the tool's AI voice for a first pass, then record your own voice for at least one beat and compare. The finished video, with captions, goes to the gallery with your beat sheet.

## Slide 3: The beat sheet

- A beat begins wherever the speaker's tactic changes
- Plead, accuse, joke, remember, decide
- Directors and actors score scripts this way; animators need the same thing

Take any speech and draw a line wherever the speaker's tactic changes: from pleading to threatening, from remembering to deciding. Each segment is a beat, and each beat gets one word for its tactic. Directors and actors score scripts this way. Animators need the same thing, because in an animation tool nothing happens unless you specify it.

## Slide 4: Animation is performance, decomposed

- Gesture and posture: open, closed, leaning, still
- Facial expression: one preset per beat
- Position and camera: wide, medium, close
- Voice: AI or your own recording; pace and pause
- The completed beat sheet is your algorithm

On stage, an actor makes hundreds of micro-decisions continuously. In an animation tool, you control four variables per beat: gesture and posture, facial expression, position and camera, and voice. That forces the computational thinking moves into the open. You decompose the speech, recognize where tactics shift, abstract each beat into four choices, and sequence them on a timeline. Students who struggle to say what an actor 'does' often find it easier when they have to tell a character to do it.

## Slide 5: Where the AI is

- Text-to-speech, automatic lip-sync, gesture suggestion
- Default delivery is even, pleasant, and dramatically empty
- Your beat sheet is what makes the performance mean something

Tools like Animaker and Synthesia offer AI text-to-speech, automatic lip-sync, and sometimes gesture suggestions. They make the avatar look alive with almost no input, and that's exactly the problem: default delivery is even, pleasant, and dramatically empty. That's why you'll record your own voice for at least one beat. Listen to the two side by side and write one sentence about the difference. Your beat sheet is what makes the performance mean something.

## Slide 6: Co-review with the beat sheet

- Watch a peer's video with their beat sheet beside you
- Where did the performance match the plan? Where did it drift?
- This is collaboration: feedback against a shared plan

When you review two classmates, watch with their beat sheet open beside the video. Say where the performance matched the plan and where it drifted. That's co-review, and it's the collaboration competency in practice: feedback against a shared plan rather than a shrug and a compliment. Use a public-domain text or your own scene from lesson two point one, keep captions on, and enjoy directing.
